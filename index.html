Studying memory in the real world

I am an MS candidate in the Department of Biomedical Engineering at Sungkyunkwan University, and Center for Neuroscience Imaging Research, advised by Drs. Min-Suk Kang and Seok-Jun Hong.

I am interested in how we remember a space, how we stack routes into coherent knowledge, and how we plan with the structure we have built. My work combines behavioral experiments and fMRI to ask what that structure looks like in the brain, and when it is brought to mind while moving toward a goal.

Memory in navigation

I have been bad with directions (still am, but I have kept improving since I started my master’s degree), and that is the reason why I entered the field. As we navigate, we have to know where we are, and knowing where we are means locating ourselves within a structure that we have learned earlier. I study how that structure is built and how it is put to use during navigation. Three projects from my undergraduate and master’s degrees approach this from different sides. The details of each project are here.

I have two main questions for future work. The first is how navigation works outside the laboratory. So far I have studied it in the scanner, where the participant lies still and the world moves on a screen. The navigation we do every day is different. You turn, you accelerate, you feel the floor under you, and those idiothetic cues, as well as other visual cues, combinatorially shape our navigational experience. I want to know whether the memory and planning we measure under laboratory conditions are the same processes operating in a richer world, and how complex experiences shape our internal representation of the outside space.

The second is how goal-directed behavior is organized at the neural level. As we navigate toward a goal, the scene we are currently seeing could be different from what we are thinking about (planning). You could picture the turn ahead, a familiar landmark, or an event that happened last week. I am very much interested in how our internal representations interact with the continuous stream of information we are currently perceiving while we produce goal-directed behavior.

How to coexist with the new species — AI

Humanity has built something close to a new species, Artificial Intelligence, and the pace of that building is faster than our understanding of it. Thus, I seriously contemplate how humans and AI actually work, which makes this moment very exciting. We now have a second system that learns from experience, generalizes, and gets things wrong in ways we can inspect line by line.

To know where you are is not only to build a map. It is to decide which map you are on. For example, a hallway in our school building looks very similar between other floors. But, realizing which floor you are becomes a different problem. Cognitive mapping literature asks how a structure is learned and that structure is generalized to similar situations. I am more interested in the moment before that: how a system picks one structure, schema, or past experience out of many and treats the current moment as an instance of it. Humans do this well under conditions that make it hard: experience is continuous, evidence is partial, and a goal is being pursued at the same time.

How does that work, and why does it work that way? I think this problem connects to other models and theories of memory and sequential decision making problem, and my dream is to build a theory of it one day!
